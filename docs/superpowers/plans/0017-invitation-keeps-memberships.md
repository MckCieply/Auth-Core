**Goal:** An invitation adds a company to an account that already exists, whatever it holds: the account keeps its other companies, its personal
space, its password, its logins and its sessions, joins with the link alone, and lands in the new company; `409 private_account_exists` is no longer
answered.

**Architecture:** `InviteAcceptance` changes its judgement from "the one space a sign-in would take" to two reads made under the locks: "a member of
the invited company?" and "the personal space, if any". Under the company lock and the address lock it now locks the account row `FOR UPDATE` for
every existing account (and, for the move only, the personal membership row before it), so a removal that would delete the account either sees the
new membership (the recount of plan 0016 Task 1) or deletes the account first and the acceptance makes a new one. A join adds the membership with
`JoinedAt = LastUsedAt = now` and changes nothing else (no new security stamp, no session ended). The provider path joins an existing account whose
identity is already its login and signs it in to the invited company; an identity that is not its login is `409 already_registered`. The preview gains
`has_account` and `org_id`. The invitation mail has one variant for every address that has an account. The sample shows an "Accept the invitation"
button for an existing account and switches a signed-in session of the same person into the new company.

**Tech Stack:** .NET 10, ASP.NET Core minimal APIs, ASP.NET Identity, OpenIddict (untouched), EF Core on PostgreSQL 16 (Npgsql), xUnit v3 with
Testcontainers; Angular 21 (standalone components, signals), Vitest on jsdom and Playwright; bash with curl and Python 3 (standard library only)
for the e2e scripts. **No new package, no migration, no new audit kind, no new operation** (spec: "No new kind"; "The counts pinned by
`OpenApiHardeningTests` do not change").

## Global Constraints

Values copied from the spec, one line each; every task includes them.

- **Preview `POST /auth/invites/preview` `{"token"}`:** `200 {"org_name","email","role","has_private_account","has_account","org_id"}`; `has_private_account` true when the account has a personal space, also with a company; `has_account` true when the address has an account of any kind; `org_id` the invited company's id; `409 already_member` only for a member of the invited company; `400 invalid_token` as today; the preview never uses the invitation.
- **Acceptance `POST /auth/invites/accept` `{"token","password"?,"leave_private_account"?}`, checks in this order:** 1. body rules of spec 0010 step 1: `400 invalid_request`; 2. unknown, used, expired, cancelled, replaced or look-alike: `400 invalid_token`; 3. a member of the invited company: `409 already_member`, the invitation stays usable; 4. an account, `leave_private_account` true, a personal space: the move, `204`; 5. an account otherwise: the join (a `password` sent is not used and not judged; `leave_private_account: true` without a personal space is ignored), `204`; 6. no account: no password `400 invalid_request`, weak `400 weak_password`, else the account is made as today (its login streak and mail links end, `AccountSessions.EndAllAsync` as today), `204`.
- **A join**, in one transaction under the locks: the invitation is used up; the membership is added with `JoinedAt` and `LastUsedAt` the time of the acceptance; an unconfirmed email is confirmed; `invite.accepted` is written. **Nothing else changes**: other memberships, the personal space, the password, the linked providers, the security stamp, the sessions, the mail links and the login streak (a lockout included).
- **The move:** a join plus the deletion of the personal space, its role row and its membership; sessions of that space get `401 invalid_grant` at their next refresh or switch; every other session goes on; `invite.accepted` carries `details.left_private_account` `true` and `details.personal_org_id`.
- **Every acceptance writes `LastUsedAt` = `JoinedAt`** (the new-account path too); on the provider path the sign-in writes it again, not earlier than `JoinedAt`.
- **Exchange, purpose `invite`, checks in this order:** 1. `400 invalid_token` as today; 2. `403 address_mismatch` as today; 3. an account under a look-alike: `400 invalid_token` as today; 4. a member of the invited company: `409 already_member`; 5. the identity is a login of another account (or there is no account and the identity is some account's login): `409 provider_already_linked`; 6. an account and the identity is not one of its logins: `409 already_registered`; 7. an account and the identity is one of its logins: the join (never the move), the ticket used up, signed in to the invited company (`login.succeeded` with `details.provider`, `200 {"status":"authenticated","access_token"}`, the refresh cookie); 8. no account: as today. In steps 3 to 6 the invitation and the ticket stay usable.

## Lock order (binding for every task)

The order of locks in one transaction is: **company lock, address lock, membership rows, account row** (plan 0015). Consequences here:

- The link acceptance and the provider acceptance take the company lock of the invited company and the address lock (as today), then, for an existing account: **only for the move** (`leave_private_account: true`), the account's personal membership rows `FOR UPDATE` (`SELECT "CompanyId" AS "Value" FROM "Memberships" WHERE "UserId" = {id} AND "CompanyId" IN (SELECT "Id" FROM "Companies" WHERE "Kind" = 2) ORDER BY "CompanyId" FOR UPDATE`; `CompanyKind.Personal` is `2`: check the enum and write it from the enum, not as a literal); then `LockAccountAsync` (the account row `FOR UPDATE`); then plain reads of the memberships. A join takes no lock on an existing membership row: it only inserts.
- A sign-in or a switch holds its membership row and then waits for the account row (`FOR KEY SHARE`). The join holds the account row and waits for no membership row, so a sign-in waits for the join and then proceeds. The move waits for a held personal membership **before** it holds the account row, so the sign-in it waits for can finish.
- A removal and a company deletion (no address lock) lock the membership rows they see, then the account rows `FOR UPDATE`, then recount (plan 0016 Task 1). The acceptance's new membership row is not visible to them before it commits. Either the acceptance holds the account row first (the removal waits at its `FOR UPDATE`, then recounts and keeps the account) or the removal holds it first (the acceptance waits at `LockAccountAsync`, finds the row gone and takes the no-account path).
- `DELETE /auth/account` takes the address lock first, so a self-deletion and an acceptance of one address run one after the other.
- `POST /auth/orgs` (plan 0016) takes the address lock and then the account row `FOR UPDATE`: the same order as the acceptance, so the two wait for each other at the address lock and never deadlock.
- The acceptance no longer deletes mail links or login streaks of an existing account, so its account row lock never waits on the mail sender's locks.

## Decisions taken in this plan

- **The recount is not built here.** It is in `AccountDeletion.DeleteAsync` since plan 0016 Task 1 (`884e713`, `AccountDeletionRecountTests`); this plan only relies on it and tests the races that need it (Task 3).
- **The personal membership is locked only for the move.** A join deletes nothing, so it takes no lock on an existing membership row; the move deletes the personal membership, so it waits for that row before it holds the account row (lock order). The lock statement runs whenever `leave_private_account` is `true` and finds zero or one row; no read before it decides.
- **Ruling (orchestrator): `TenancyErrors.PrivateAccountExists` is deleted** with its `StatusOf` arm and its names in `.ProducesError`, so `StatusOf("private_account_exists")` throws (tested in Task 4; the link acceptance stops answering it in Task 2, the provider path in Task 4); the sample keeps the string in `auth.service.ts` for an older server. - Why: no path may answer the code (criterion 13), and a constant left unused could be sent again by a later change without any test failing. - Cost if wrong: a third-party client that compiles against the constant would need its own string; the server's answers are the same either way.
- **The email confirmation is saved through the tracked entity** in the acceptance's `SaveChangesAsync`, not through `UserManager.UpdateAsync` (which would also renormalise the address and write a new concurrency stamp) and not through `UpdateSecurityStampAsync` (which would end the sessions Decision 3 keeps). A test reads it back from the database.
- **`InvitePreview` and `InvitePreviewResponse` gain `HasAccount` and `OrgId` at the end**, as the spec says; `HasPrivateAccount` keeps its place.
- **The sample compares addresses up to ASCII case only**, as the server does (`EmailInput.IsSameAddress`), not with `toLowerCase()`.
- **Ruling (orchestrator): an older server's preview without `org_id`** (`null` in the sample): the sample does not switch and opens `/login` with the email, as with no session (Task 6). - Why: without the company's id the sample cannot know where to switch, and `/login` with the address filled in is the path that always works. - Cost if wrong: a signed-in person on an older server signs in once more instead of landing in the new company at once.
- **After `switchTo` answers `'signed-out'`** (its own navigation to `/login` has no email), the invite page navigates to `/login` with the email state afterwards, so the address is filled in.
- **The changed race tests keep their cases and change their hooks** (`HeldLock.AccountRowAsync` in place of `HeldLock.MembershipsAsync`; the acceptance sent as a task in `PrivateAccountRaceTests` :116), as the spec's "To verify" says; their names change to say the new order.
- **The Playwright test of an account with a company** invites from a company the seed user creates through `POST /auth/orgs` (plan 0016), because `inviteByApi` invites only into the development company and the seed user is not an admin of Beta; it deletes that company afterwards.
- **Ruling (orchestrator): the `remove-member` CLI race is tested in Task 3** (`InvitationJoinRaceTests.An_acceptance_that_holds_the_account_row_keeps_the_account_from_remove_member_and_the_cli_says_kept`). - Why: criterion 18 names what `remove-member` says, and the CLI runs the removal through its own path, so the HTTP removal test does not cover it. - Cost if wrong: one more race test (a few seconds of the suite); without it the CLI could say "deleted" for a kept account unnoticed.
- **Ruling (orchestrator): each task's commit is green; the provider path keeps its answers until Task 4 through a shim** (Task 2 reads the space with `SpaceOfAsync` for `AcceptThroughProviderAsync` only; the tests whose hooks or answers Task 2 changes move into Task 2). - Why: a red commit breaks review and bisect. - Cost if wrong: a few lines of shim, removed in Task 4.
